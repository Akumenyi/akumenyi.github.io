There is a version of the solar geoengineering debate that can be conducted entirely
in global means. Inject sulphate aerosol into the stratosphere, reflect a fraction of
incoming sunlight, hold global-mean surface temperature at some target. On that
framing the question is settled enough to argue about on a conference panel.

The trouble is that nobody lives in a global mean.

## What the resolution hides

Most of the model output the field reasons from — including the ensembles I work
with — runs at grid spacings of roughly 100 km. A single grid cell at that
resolution can swallow a coastline, an escarpment, a city and the farmland that
feeds it, and return one number for all of them. For a question about the planetary
energy budget, that is an acceptable trade. For a question about whether the rains
arrive on time in a specific farming district, it is the wrong instrument.

This matters more for intervention scenarios than for ordinary warming scenarios,
because the quantity people care about is a *difference* between two futures. Small
regional biases that are tolerable when you are looking at a trend become the whole
signal when you are differencing two runs.

## Where the signal actually lives

In our work on the ARISE-SAI ensemble, the response of African precipitation
extremes to stratospheric aerosol injection is not well summarised by the change in
mean rainfall, and it is not uniform across the continent. Cooling the planet and
restoring its climate are not the same intervention, and the gap between them shows
up in the tails — in wet-day intensity, in dry-spell length, in the timing of onset.

> A deployment that restores global-mean temperature can still redistribute heavy
> rainfall, lengthen dry spells, or move the moisture a monsoon depends on.

That is an argument for higher resolution, not for more caveats. Dynamical
downscaling through CESM regional refinement lets us raise spatial and vertical
resolution over a chosen region while keeping the global circulation coupled and
consistent — which is the combination you need if the answer is going to be both
locally meaningful and physically honest.

## Why this is a governance problem too

The resolution question is usually filed under model development. I think it belongs
in the governance conversation as well. If the evidence base available to an African
delegation consists of coarse global fields, then the terms of the debate are set by
what the models happen to resolve, and the regions with the most at stake arrive with
the least usable evidence.

Producing regionally refined assessments is not a technical nicety. It is a
precondition for anyone being able to say no on the basis of evidence — or yes.
