What I work on

Four questions driving the work

Climate intervention is moving from thought experiment to policy conversation faster than the regional evidence base can keep up. My work tries to close that gap — at the resolution where people actually live.

01

What does stratospheric aerosol injection do to extremes?

Global-mean cooling says almost nothing about the tails. Using ARISE-SAI and CESM2, I ask how the distribution of heavy rainfall, dry spells and heat shifts under deployment — and how that differs from the baseline climate people are already adapting to.

Read more {% include icon.html name="arrow" %}
02

Can we see intervention at regional scale?

A 100 km grid cell is the wrong instrument for a question about a district. I use CESM regional refinement to downscale SAI experiments, so the answers arrive at a scale a national planner can act on.

Read more {% include icon.html name="arrow" %}
03

How do large-scale processes co-behave?

Subtropical highs, monsoon onset and moisture transport do not act one at a time. My thesis work built indices and a co-behaviour method that treat them as a system — and used it to interrogate how well GCMs reproduce it.

Read more {% include icon.html name="arrow" %}
04

Who gets to decide?

Africa carries the sharpest end of climate risk and the thinnest seat at the governance table. Alongside the modelling, I work on the case for African-led governance of solar radiation modification, with UNEP, WMO, TWAS and ICLEI.
